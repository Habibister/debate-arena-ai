## 1. Executive summary

**CompeteReady has excellent learning *infrastructure* and very little learning *content*.**

The engine is real and honest. Practice sessions are server-bound and tamper-resistant (M13E2),
mastery requires evidence floors and must survive a 1/3/7/14-day spaced ladder, provenance is
labelled, and the product refuses to fake progress. That machinery is genuinely good and was built
carefully.

What runs on top of it is thin:

- **7 lessons are learner-visible across all three tracks** — 5 Debate, 1 DECA, 1 HOSA — and the DECA
  and HOSA ones are "how this event works" orientation, not skill instruction. **DECA and HOSA have
  zero concept skill lessons live.**
- **12 authored lessons are sitting dormant** in `lib/learning-content.ts` — each with a worked
  example, a hint, a practice set and a mastery check — because they were never added to the
  education registry. Three are DECA, three are HOSA. They are **thin**, though: roughly two
  sentences of explanation, 3–4 steps, one weak/strong pair, one guided question, three practice
  questions and one mastery item. Registering them as-is triples the thin-lesson problem rather than
  solving it — worth doing, but behind a locked template.
- **The entire drill corpus is 126 questions: exactly 9 per area.** A learner who asks for a
  20-question drill sees 9 distinct questions padded out to 20 slots. That is not a design quirk —
  it is the bank running out.
- **20 video lessons are fully scripted, with 22 interactive pause-point questions, and not one has
  been produced.** There is no video player, no diagram, no illustration anywhere in the product.
- **There is no mistake bank and no targeted retry.** `QuestionAttempt` rows are written by exactly
  one route and never read back. Spaced review re-serves a *random* rep of a skill, not the items the
  learner actually missed.
- **Lessons are single-scroll articles.** Both lesson renderers are server components with no step
  state; every section renders at once.

And — the finding that matters most, because it cuts against the product's central promise —
**several honesty rules are violated in shipped code**:

- **`MASTERED` is granted on the first passing session**, with no spaced survival required
  (`lib/spaced-review.ts:305-309,531`). Five correct questions writes `MASTERED` at 100% with a
  review due tomorrow. The ladder only governs whether that number can later go *down*. The
  percentage is an upward-only high-water mark (`:543-545`).
- **A DECA ballot generated by the local fallback — hardcoded `[4,3,4,3,4]` scores, canned
  strengths, nothing derived from the learner's transcript — is stamped with the official registry
  spec** (`lib/ai.ts:1824,1846-1848`). The learner sees "78 overall — Scored against Hotel and
  Lodging Management Series 2025-2026" with only a small "Using backup AI response." note.
- **`/study-arcade` tells learners "every arcade rep updates your real mastery progress"**
  (`page.tsx:114`) while `components/study/` makes **zero** network calls and
  `lib/coach-progress.ts:178-180` states in code that no server-side flashcard progress exists.
- **Three of four DECA drill areas have no `Skill` row**, so drilling them records nothing
  (`prisma/seed.ts` seeds only `deca-marketing`). To the product's credit the UI says "Not tracked
  yet" rather than faking it — but three quarters of DECA drilling is inert.
- **The HOSA clinical judging that M11R6 deliberately withdrew is still reachable.**
  `/api/ai/judge-hosa` returns a principled 410, but `POST /api/debates` accepts
  `organization: "HOSA"` unguarded and the debate judge route dispatches it straight to
  `judgeHosaPerformance` — then persists the score and awards XP, wins and streak
  (`app/api/debates/[debateId]/judge/route.ts:199-206,287,306-313`). This is the audit's most
  serious finding.

Genuinely strong and not to be disturbed: the **full Debate round** (staged speeches, timers, AI
opponent, judged ballot, saved to the learner's record), the **M13E2 session integrity work**, the
**fail-closed discipline**, and the **provenance labelling**. The honesty *architecture* is
excellent; several honesty *behaviours* have drifted from it.

**Honest completion against the full definition of a beginner-to-championship learning product: ~30%.**
Weighted toward content and teaching quality as instructed, not infrastructure. The infrastructure
alone would score far higher; that is exactly why it must not be the basis of the estimate.

**And the first mile is broken.** `app/api/signup/route.ts:43` stores the organization a learner
picks at signup, and **nothing ever reads it to set their track**: `lib/track-server.ts` resolves
only from `?track=` or the track cookie, and the cookie is written solely by the client switcher,
which initialises to `DEFAULT_TRACK = GENERAL_DEBATE`. A student who signs up choosing DECA or HOSA
lands in General Debate. Signup also offers **Public Speaking**, which has no track at all in
`lib/training-tracks.ts` and no registered lessons — a dead end from the first click.

That changes the recommended first move. Registering dormant lessons is still cheap and valuable, but
it puts content behind a door two thirds of new learners never open. **Fix the track routing first.**

## 2. Existing research and decisions found

### 2.1 What the repository actually establishes

The authoritative pedagogy model is in `docs/COMPETEREADY_MASTER_PLAN.md:68-70`:

> Competition → Event → Round Structure → Rubric → Skill → Lesson → Drill → Attempt → Feedback →
> Redo → Transfer → Spaced Reassessment. Every learning feature must locate itself on this chain.
> Mastery is earned and must survive spaced reassessment to count.

That twelve-link chain — not any other framing — is the established requirement. The roadmap
(`docs/COMPETEREADY_MASTER_PLAN.md:44-52`) lists six items, of which three bear directly on this
audit:

| # | Roadmap item | State per this audit |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **Rubric Engine** — judges score against registry category points, not hardcoded seeds | Partially connected. DECA role-play judging reads the spec registry (`lib/ai.ts:1787`); Debate judging does not. |
| 3 | **Study Arcade depth** — question-bank breadth; unified drills | **Not done.** 9 questions per area. |
| 4 | **Track Simulations** — full timed rounds per track from registry structure | Debate only. DECA's full simulation saves nothing; HOSA's is labelled "Not built yet". |

The curriculum corpus (`docs/curriculum/`, 15 files, ~3,700 lines) is a genuine research record with
sourcing discipline, per-track course designs, three benchmark lessons, and a final synthesis
(`14-final-synthesis.md`). `docs/curriculum/04-practice-progress-engines.md` establishes the
universal practice block, redo-as-completion, and that guided practice writes nothing.

## 4. Lesson-quality rubric

A lesson counts as **complete** only if it has all thirteen. A title, or an article, counts as zero.

| # | Element | What satisfies it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clear beginner explanation | Plain-language definition assuming no prior knowledge |
| 2 | Manageable instructional sections | Content broken into steps the learner advances through — not one scroll |
| 3 | Examples | At least one concrete instance of the skill |
| 4 | Worked demonstration | A problem solved *in front of* the learner, showing the reasoning |
| 5 | Guided practice | Learner attempts with scaffolding still present |
| 6 | Hints | Available on demand before the answer is revealed |
| 7 | Immediate corrective feedback | Explains *why*, not just right/wrong |
| 8 | Independent practice | Scaffolding removed |
| 9 | Targeted remediation | A miss routes to the specific material that fixes it |
| 10 | Mastery assessment on NEW material | Not a repeat of practice items |
| 11 | Spaced review | The skill returns after a gap, gated on real evidence |
| 12 | Real competition application | Connects to how the event is actually run and scored |
| 13 | Visual or video support | Where it meaningfully improves learning |

## 6. Flashcard audit

Against the Knowt-style standard in the brief. Code: `components/study/flashcard-study.tsx` (214
lines), `components/study/games/*`, `lib/study-content.ts` (1,260 lines).

| Front/back interaction | **Y** | `setFlipped` toggle, `flashcard-study.tsx:148` |
| Know it / Still learning / Don't know | **P** | Only **two** controls: `markKnown` (:63) and `markLearning` (:73). No third "Don't know" state. |
| New / Learning / Review / Mastered states | **P** | Three buckets only — known / learning / remaining (:28-29). No Review state, no Mastered vs Known distinction. |

| Test mode | **N** | No `testMode` anywhere in the repo |
| Mixed recall + multiple choice | **P** | `multiple-choice-game.tsx` and `tile-match-game.tsx` exist as *games*, separate from the study flow; the card flow itself is recall-only |
| Explanations and examples | **P** | The data has `beginnerExplanation`, `example`, `commonMistake`, `quickCheck` — but `example` and `commonMistake` are **templated**: every DECA card reads "In a DECA roleplay, bring up ⟨term⟩ when you explain the problem…". `quickCheckAnswer` is a **verbatim copy of `definition`**, so the quick check tests nothing. |

### 6.1 The two findings that matter

**Self-declared mastery is displayed as a mastery percentage.**
`flashcard-study.tsx:28` computes `masteryPercent = knownIds.length / cards.length`, where `knownIds`
is populated purely by the learner pressing "Got it". It renders as **"Term mastery — N%"** with a
progress bar (:136-141). Clicking through a deck pressing "Got it" drives that bar to 100% without
answering anything.

To be fair and precise: this is **device-local only** (`localStorage`, :40-56), it never reaches the
server, and it does not touch XP, rank, `MasteryProgress` or the spaced ladder. So it does not
corrupt real mastery. But the product rule is *"no invented percentages"*
(`COMPETEREADY_MASTER_PLAN.md:24-26`), and a bar labelled "Term mastery" that responds to clicking is
in tension with it.

**Debate has no flashcards at all.** The `Flashcard` type literally excludes it:
`organization: Exclude<StudyOrganization, "DEBATE">` (`lib/study-content.ts:7`). A Debate learner
opening Study finds nothing for their track.

### 6.2 One thing already right

`components/study/games/missed-terms-drill.tsx` is a genuine targeted-remediation pattern: it takes
the cards a learner missed and requires `DRILL_REQUIRED_CORRECT` correct answers on each before
releasing them. **The pattern is already proven in this codebase.** Its only limitation is that
`missedCards` is `useState` in `study-games.tsx:34` — in-memory, per-session, lost on reload. It is
not a persisted mistake bank. That is a small distance to cover for a large gain.

### 7.1 The calibration gap

`judge-shape:smoke` (`scripts/judge-shape-smoke.ts:78-82`) **exits 0 with only a console warning**
when no provider responds after four attempts — a green result can mean the live check was skipped.
That is documented in `docs/HANDOFF.md`, and it is the only judge-quality check that exists. There is
no test that the same input produces a consistent score, and no reference set of human-scored
artifacts to calibrate against. For a product whose promise is honest scoring against real rubrics,
this is the most important AI gap.

## 10. Beginner-to-championship learner-journey audit

Walking the path a genuine beginner takes, per track.

### 10.1 Debate — the only track with a real path, and it still has a cliff

1. `/onboarding/diagnostic` — works: the form writes a `LearningProfile`, and the dashboard reads it
   and derives a starting skill via `scoreDiagnostic()` (`app/(app)/dashboard/page.tsx:239`). It is
   `localStorage`-only, so it does not follow the learner across devices (G15).
2. `/training/debate` → CWI lesson → 4 concept lessons. Good teaching, but they scroll, and **their
   practice records nothing**, so the learner's effort does not enter the mastery chain.
3. Drills — 36 questions. A learner who drills seriously exhausts an area in one sitting.
4. `/debate` — a full staged, timed, judged round. **This is the product's best moment.**
5. Championship: `/debates/history` and replay. No attempt comparison, no motion library beyond
   `lib/debate-topics.ts`, no tournament simulation, no ladder.

**The cliff:** between "I finished the lessons" and "I am in a full round" there is nothing but 36
multiple-choice questions. No graduated speech drills, no partial-round practice, no rebuttal-only
reps.

### 10.2 DECA — no instructional on-ramp

1. `/training/deca` → Navigator (8 families, all routed, honestly labelled).
2. One orientation lesson explaining how role-play works.
3. 36 concept drill questions.
4. `/training/deca/practice` → role-play room. **Saves nothing** (`roleplay-config.ts:4`).
5. `/compete` offers "Full DECA Simulation — Results aren't saved yet".

**The gap:** a beginner is told how role-play works, then put in one, with no lesson teaching them to
read a scenario, find the root problem, or sound like an advisor — **despite all three lessons being
written and sitting dormant.**

### 10.3 HOSA — the thinnest path

1. `/training/hosa` → Event Navigator (8 events).
2. **7 of 8 events have no practice.** Only `medical-terminology` routes anywhere.
3. Medical Terminology: 54 questions, review-only, honest thresholds. Solid as far as it goes.
4. Communication lesson: informational, its interactive scenario `temporarily-unavailable`.
5. `/compete`: "Full HOSA Simulation — Not built yet — coming soon."

**The gap:** for seven of eight HOSA events, CompeteReady tells a learner what the event is and then
offers nothing to train it. The three authored HOSA lessons are dormant.

### 10.4 Cross-cutting journey breaks

- **Two disconnected practice systems.** The M13E2 server-bound system records evidence, mastery and
  review. The concept-education practice (`concept-education-lesson-practice.tsx`) records nothing.
  The four newest Debate lessons use the second one. A learner cannot tell which of their effort
  counts.
- **No mistake bank.** `QuestionAttempt` is written by exactly one route
  (`app/api/skills/debate-writing/route.ts:144`) and **never read by any query** — no `findMany`,
  `findFirst`, `groupBy` or `count` anywhere in `app/`, `lib/` or `components/`.
- **No targeted retry.** `/study-arcade/review` re-serves a *skill*, not the items missed. Its own
  comment says it links "into the EXISTING skill practice flow (no new drill types)".
- **Spaced review can't bite with a 9-question bank.** Intervals are 1/3/7/14 days
  (`lib/spaced-review.ts:13`). By the 7-day review a learner has likely seen all 9 questions in an
  area more than once, so the reassessment measures recall of specific items, not the skill.

## 15. Definition of done for the complete educational product

CompeteReady is a complete beginner-to-championship learning platform when **all** of the following
hold, verified by suite or preview, not by assertion:

1. **Every track has a complete path** — a beginner can go from zero knowledge to a scored full
   simulation without leaving the product, in Debate, DECA and HOSA.
2. **Every learner-visible lesson scores 13/13** on the section 4 rubric, or its missing elements are
   deliberate and documented.
3. **No authored content is dormant** — everything written is either reachable or explicitly held
   with a recorded reason.
4. **Question banks support the review ladder** — enough items per area that a full 1/3/7/14 cycle
   does not exhaust the pool.
5. **Misses are captured and re-served.** A learner can see what they got wrong and practise exactly
   that.
5b. **Every mastery label is provable from stored history** — `MASTERED` never appears without passed
   due reassessments, and no percentage is a best-ever high-water mark.
5c. **No fabricated output carries official attribution** — a fallback ballot shows no score and no
   spec, and every learner-facing progress claim is true of the surface that makes it.
6. **One practice system.** Every practice surface either records to the mastery chain or tells the
   learner plainly that it does not.
7. **Lessons are stepped, not scrolled.**
8. **Flashcards meet the standard** — four states, card-level scheduling, Learn and Test modes, mixed
   recall and multiple choice, per-card examples, lesson links, and **no percentage derived from
   self-report**.
9. **AI judging is calibrated** — reference ballots, score-band assertions, a consistency check, and
   a judge test that fails loudly rather than exiting 0 when a provider is down.
10. **All judging is registry-grounded** — categories and weights come from `CompetitionSpec`, never
    hardcoded seeds.
11. **Visual instruction exists** where the curriculum argues it materially helps, with text
    equivalents.
12. **Every track has a persisted full simulation** with history and replay, like Debate's today.
13. **Every honesty rule still holds** — no fake progress, no fake official content, labelled AI,
    track isolation, provenance, fail-closed unknowns.
14. **Accessibility and mobile hold throughout** — status never colour-alone, keyboard reachable,
    reduced motion respected.
15. **`npm run build` plus all safe smoke suites are green**, and behaviour is verified in a running
    environment.

harshly to score the curriculum delta. **Treat the honest range as 25–30%**, and note that the

3. The first mile is broken in a way that makes most of the rest unreachable, and that is the

Phase 1 alone — a track-resolution fix, one authorized script run, and registering lessons that are
already written — would route DECA and HOSA learners to their own tracks, turn three quarters of DECA
drilling from inert into real mastery, and take DECA from zero skill lessons to three. None of it
