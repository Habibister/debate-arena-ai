Status: **DRAFT V2** — revised after the complete research-validation program (official sources,
Reddit anecdote, caption analysis) and the approved Final Research Synthesis + Correction Addendum.
The Correction Addendum controls wherever wording conflicts with the original synthesis.
Authored by Claude, 2026-07-29; revised 2026-07-30. No code implements this yet.

## 0. Who this curriculum is written for

The modal learner is a **coachless or small-program competitor**: no dedicated coach, a stretched
advisor, few or no teammates, and no access to a circuit's institutional knowledge. This was the most
consistently observed condition across every anecdotal pass, and it sets product defaults:

- Every lesson must stand alone without a coach present to interpret it.
- Practice must be **solo-usable** (record → self-grade against a visible rubric).
- Anything that assumes a partner, a squad room, or a judge on hand is optional enrichment, never a
  prerequisite.
- Procedural/orientation knowledge ("what actually happens at a tournament or conference") is treated
  as first-class content, not a footnote — competitors without programs report losing points to
  procedure, not just to skill.

## 1. Research-informed teaching principles

Every CompeteReady lesson, drill, and simulation follows these principles. Each principle names the
learning-science basis and the product behavior it requires.

1. **Worked examples before production** (worked-example effect, Sweller). A beginner sees a complete
   expert performance — annotated line by line — before being asked to produce anything. Product rule:
   no lesson asks for learner output before showing a full model.
2. **Weak-vs-strong contrast on the SAME task** (contrasting cases, Schwartz & Bransford). The weak and
   strong versions must share the same scenario/motion so the difference is attributable to skill, not
   topic. Product rule: never contrast a weak example on one topic with a strong example on another.
3. **Annotation at the line level.** The learning is in *why* each line succeeds or fails, not in the
   transcript itself. Every worked example carries per-line annotations.
4. **Fading scaffolds** (guidance fading). Sequence within any skill: full model → completion task
   (learner fills one missing part) → guided production with rubric visible → independent production →
   timed/pressured production. Product rule: the practice engine (doc 04) encodes these five rungs.
5. **Retrieval practice over re-reading** (testing effect). Review sessions ask the learner to produce
   or choose, never just re-display the lesson.
6. **Spaced review with a DIFFERENT example** (spacing + variability). Mastery counts only if it
   survives spaced reassessment on material not previously seen — this is already CompeteReady's
   "no fake progress" rule; the curriculum extends it: the review item must be a *new* instance of the
   concept, not the original question re-shown.
7. **Immediate, specific feedback tied to the learner's actual words.** Feedback quotes or closely
   paraphrases what the learner wrote/said. Generic feedback ("be more specific") is a defect.
8. **Immediate redo** (deliberate practice, Ericsson). After feedback, the learner revises the same
   response before moving on. The redo — not the first attempt — is the completion event of a practice
   block. Independently corroborated as the single most-endorsed habit in the anecdotal record.
9. **Error classification** (error taxonomies). Misses are tagged with a named error category
   (per-track taxonomies in doc 04) so recurrence is visible and recommendations target the pattern,
   not the single miss.
10. **Transfer to unseen material.** A skill "counts" when demonstrated on a scenario/question the
    learner has not seen. Seen-accuracy and unseen-accuracy are tracked separately (doc 04).
11. **Interleaving.** Once 2+ skills are learned, practice sets mix them, forcing the learner to choose
    the right tool rather than pattern-match the session topic.
12. **Simulation AFTER skill acquisition, and diagnostic in purpose.** Full simulations are placed at
    module ends and used to reveal weaknesses that route back into lessons/drills. A simulation is
    never the teaching instrument for a skill the learner hasn't been taught.
13. **Performance is taught, not assumed.** Delivery, poise, in-character behavior, and audience
    adaptation get their own lessons, worked examples, and drills — knowledge/terminology are support
    layers that feed performance (the Knowledge-to-Performance Bridge, docs 02/03).
14. **Video self-review** (simulation-education debriefing practice): staged review — audio only →
    muted video → full recording → immediate corrected re-recording — so the learner attends to one
    channel at a time. See §8 for the privacy gates on any learner-generated recording.
15. **Advocacy is a role.** Debate teaches steelmanning and ethical advocacy of an assigned side;
    never lying, invented evidence, or misrepresentation (doc 01, Module 1).

## 2. Evidence hierarchy (authoritative order)

This hierarchy governs every claim in every curriculum document and every lesson.

1. **Current official governing sources** — NSDA manual/ballots/paradigm standards; DECA Guide,
   evaluation forms, blueprints, PI lists; HOSA event guidelines + rating sheets + GRR + appendices.
   These ALONE control event structure, timing, scoring, allowed procedures, required steps,
   professional boundaries, safety, and event terminology.
2. **Official instructional or page-description sources** — official teaching guides, lesson plans,
   event pages, sample indexes. Control pedagogy framing and official emphasis; never override tier 1.
3. **Approved official-source synthesis findings** — the C/D/H change-log entries produced by the
   official research passes.
4. **Repeated anecdotal findings** — recurring competitor friction (3+ independent discussions).
   Establish **what confuses learners and where they plateau**. Never a rule, a scoring fact, or an
   effectiveness claim.
5. **Isolated anecdotal findings** — fewer than 3 independent discussions. May motivate an FAQ line;
   never a lesson's foundation.
6. **Caption-analyzed examples** — observations from actual timestamped caption text of a public asset.
   Supply **examples and supporting evidence only**, never general rules.
7. **Metadata-only or retrieval-method findings** — establish **no behavior** whatsoever.
8. **Product hypotheses** — CompeteReady's own design inferences, always labeled as ours.

**Standing rules.**

- Official evidence controls rules, timing, scoring, safety, format, terminology, and event structure.
- Anecdote may establish recurring learner friction, not rules.
- Caption analysis may supply examples or supporting evidence, not general rules.
- Metadata-only and null passes establish no behavior.
- **A single asset may never independently require a curriculum change.**
- **Absence of evidence is never converted into evidence of absence.**
- Provisional questions are never resolved by intuition.

### Currency labels (applied to every source and asset)

- **CURRENT** — from the current competitive season, confirmed by the document's own date.
- **STABLE-TEACHING** — undated or older, but pedagogically durable; **never a rules source**.
- **POSSIBLY-OUTDATED** — predates the current season, or its currency cannot be confirmed.

The **document's own date confirms currency — never the URL path**. Site labels and upload paths lag
and mislead; the "2014-form trap" is the canonical failure mode.

### Source-accessibility statuses (applied to every external video or media asset)

- **caption analyzed** — timestamped caption text was actually retrieved and analysed.
- **captions declared but not retrievable** — a track is declared; retrieval returned nothing usable.
- **no captions declared** — the player exposes no text track.
- **metadata only** — title/uploader/date/description only; establishes no behavior.
- **rights/embedding review required** — before any use beyond a plain link.

**Required functionality must never depend on third-party caption availability.** Provide fallbacks.
Do not assume external captions will always be unavailable — re-test after meaningful platform or
source changes. **Never reconstruct a copyrighted transcript manually as a workaround.**

### Already sourced in the registry (verified 2026-07-05, re-verify each season)

- PF round order + times (4/4/3 crossfire/4/4/3/3/3 grand/2/2; 3-min prep per team).
- **Speaker points: official ballots show a 20–30 descriptor scale, with sub-20 reserved as an
  unethical-conduct flag.** No current-season public scale document was located. *(Draft V1's
  "0–30" was incorrect and is withdrawn.)*
- DECA Individual Series event shape: 100-q cluster exam + 10-min prep + up-to-10-min role-play
  meeting. Per-category point splits are documented per form generation (doc 02); **seeding the
  registry remains a separate approval-gated task.**
- HOSA Medical Terminology: 50 questions / 60 minutes (changed this season from 100/90).

### Known NOT sourced (placeholder — do not teach as fact)

- **Parliamentary debate has no official source.** NSDA does not govern it. Its structure and rules
  require a dedicated sourcing pass (NPDA/state leagues) or must stay explicitly placeholder.
- Model UN conference specifics (track soft-removed).
- Non-Hospitality DECA clusters; non-MT HOSA categories (labeled generic practice).
- **The DECA Quiz Bowl document is NOT in this repository.** Nothing may be built from it until it
  exists and passes the intake pipeline (doc 02).

### Source-specific note on Claim/Warrant/Impact

**Claim/Warrant/Impact is an officially supported three-part model in the specific Debate Training
Guide.** The reviewed official corpus **also contains compatible four-part Claim/Data/Warrant/Impact
presentations.** CompeteReady keeps CWI as its beginner model and **cites the Debate Training Guide
specifically**. Curriculum and lessons **must not imply that NSDA publishes only one formulation**.

## 3. Freshness calendars (per organization)

**Debate (NSDA).** The manual is a live versioned document — cite the version in the document header,
not the site label. Re-verify at manual rollover (~July/August); PF topics monthly after Nov/Dec; LD
bimonthly; Policy annual (January); Big Questions from Aug 1.

**DECA.** Annual cadence: early-May DECA Direct updates article (structural changes are pre-announced
about a year ahead) → June Guide excerpts → July PI lists and exam blueprints → September district
instructional areas → February–April ICDC documents. **Two evaluation-form generations can be live
simultaneously.** Watch for stale-URL traps.

**HOSA.** HOSA publishes annual competitive-event guidelines. **The official site states that the
final 2026–27 guidelines are expected September 1, 2026.** Current 2025–26 details must be
revalidated against that release and against later update notices. **Do not convert one documented
release date into a permanent annual release rule.** Mid-season revisions occur; re-poll monthly
October–May. Preliminary announcements are **not** final rules.

## 4. Association and competition-level variation

- **HOSA** guidelines are written for the International Leadership Conference; **chartered
  associations may modify implementation.** State and regional administration may differ, and a local
  difference is not evidence that the ILC guideline is wrong.
- **DECA** advancement allocations and event-entry limits are association-controlled. Some
  requirements are level-specific — for example, the official blazer requirement documented at ICDC.
  **Not all chartered associations offer all events.**
- **Debate** norms vary by circuit; ballot instructions may be dated and are not automatically current
  universal rules.

**Never teach as universal:** any cut line, normalization or sectioning mechanic, advancement model,
entry limit, question-skipped penalty, device deduction, or dress requirement. Where a rule is
level-specific, say which level. Where an association decides, say so.

## 5. Attribution plan (learner-facing)

- Official-fact surfaces: "[Org] [Season] guidelines — last verified [date]", with partial/unverified
  specs flagged.
- Every authored lesson carries a provenance note: original instruction, not official material;
  scenarios illustrative; real events use official documents.
- AI-generated content (opponent turns, feedback) is always labeled AI-generated.
- Sourced structural facts inherit registry attribution rather than restating a hardcoded copy.
- **Every lesson carries a source-tier tag** — OFFICIAL / STABLE-TEACHING / TIER-2 — so learners can
  see what is a rule and what is our teaching.
- **Format tagging**: where a term means different things across formats or event families, the
  format is named inline. Cross-format contamination is the most common observed confusion mechanism.

## 6. Practice-item policy (AI-created and CompeteReady-authored)

Any practice item CompeteReady generates or authors must be:

- **Original.** Never copying official questions, paid references, textbooks, or third-party study
  sets.
- **Labeled** AI-generated or CompeteReady-created.
- **Weighted only from current official test plans and blueprints.**
- **Never presented as official, released, or predictive of competition questions.**

Officially released sample exams (where an organization publishes them) are a legitimate practice
source. Competitors' reported difficulty locating suitable official practice items is a **reported
access problem, not proof that no official materials exist anywhere.**

## 7. Copyright, linking, and embedding

- **Never host, reproduce, provide, or link to unauthorized copies.**
- **Never route around payment, authentication, licensing, or access controls.** Licensed or gated
  member content is never scraped.
- **Lawful links to official publishers, authorized retailers, or library/school catalogs are
  permitted.** Linking to an official source is a different act from reproducing it.
- **Teach from official reference lists and test plans without copying the protected source content.**
- Never copy text from official guides, test banks, sample role-plays, paid courses, or videos.
  Structure/format facts (times, step order, category names) are used as facts with attribution;
  expression is always ours.
- All scenarios, examples, transcripts, questions, and video scripts are original works authored for
  CompeteReady.
- **Third-party video: no downloading, no re-hosting, no full-transcript reproduction, no reproduction
  of substantial copyrighted speech.** Never imply CompeteReady created an official asset.
  **Embedding an official player requires a later rights, terms-of-use, technical, and product
  review — no research pass reached any legal conclusion about embedding.** The video curriculum
  (docs 05–07) is recorded by the user from our original scripts.
- Anecdotal and caption-derived insights are used as patterns, never quoted at length.

## 8. Clinical, medical, privacy, and legal scope

**Medical and clinical scope.** CompeteReady **never teaches, scores, or simulates hands-on clinical
procedures**, never reproduces or summarises procedure steps, never evaluates whether a clinical
action is medically correct, and **never claims that app practice creates clinical readiness.** It
coaches the **communication, knowledge, and rating-sheet layers only.**

**Official HOSA safety distinctions (stated as HOSA's).**

- **Live patients or actors may be used for noninvasive components where the current event guideline
  and equipment list specify them.**
- **Invasive, medication, blood-draw, injection, and resuscitation components use appropriate
  manikins, training arms, medication trainers, or other specified simulation equipment.**
- **The exact current event guideline and equipment list control.**

**Supervision guidance is CompeteReady policy, labeled as ours** — HOSA publishes no supervision
requirement, and we must never attribute one to it.

**Privacy and legal gates.** No medical, privacy, or legal wording is ever approved through anecdotal
or caption evidence. Content carrying medical, privacy, or legal claims requires **clinical and legal
review** before release. A clearly non-medical, non-legal administrative communication scenario still
requires **advisor, safety, and product review**. The HOSA clinic-privacy scenario is **disabled by
default at M3** pending an approved replacement (docs 03, 07, 12, 14).

**Learner-generated recordings and transcripts (hypothesis only).** A learner recording or
transcribing their own practice may offer a self-review pathway where third-party material is
unavailable. **Not approved. Requires review of feasibility, transcription accuracy, cost, consent,
privacy, retention, accessibility, and age-appropriate safeguarding. Students must never be required
to upload identifiable recordings. Transcript feedback must never be presented as equivalent to
audiovisual delivery feedback.** Especially sensitive for HOSA, where practice may involve clinical
settings and third parties.

## 9. Factual-accuracy safeguards

- Any event-structure/scoring/procedure claim is either (a) backed by the registry with sourced
  provenance, or (b) written in hedged, structure-light form ("your event's official guide states the
  exact times — check it") until sourced. Authored lessons never invent numbers.
- Medical content: authored, bounded, rubric-based. The AI character never diagnoses, recommends
  treatment, invents permissions/procedures, or overrides the official guideline. Patient-facing
  language pattern: observe → plain-language explanation → report to the appropriate professional.
- Business content: definitions checked against current mainstream usage; anything cluster-specific
  defers to sourced instructional-area materials.
- Every lesson passes a pre-ship review: product-reviewer (honesty/track isolation) + a
  subject-accuracy pass, per the multi-agent review rules. The agent that writes content never acts as
  its own final reviewer.
