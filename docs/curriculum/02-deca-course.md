## Event-family scope (read first)

⟨D3/D14/D22⟩ **This course teaches the role-play and case-study families only.** DECA also runs
**prepared, written, and online events** (business operations research, project management, integrated
marketing, entrepreneurship, professional selling, stock-market and virtual-business challenges) whose
rules differ materially — statements of assurance, penalty points, page and slide limits, different
weighting, and **visual aids that are permitted where role-play families prohibit them.** Those
families are **out of this course's scope**; the Navigator routes them. **Not all chartered
associations offer all events.**

## DECA Performance Course

### Module 0 — Event Orientation
*Outcome: knows their family, its components, and how scores combine. Prerequisite: none.
Applies to: all DECA. Classification: competition orientation.*

- **DECA Event Navigator** ⟨NEW — D3/D14/D22⟩ — the learner selects their exact event before any
  training is recommended. Fields: family · components · timing · exam weighting · PI count ·
  materials rules · **association availability warning** · what CompeteReady can train today.
  **Fail closed:** an unsourced event shows an honest partial card, never invented detail.
- **0. How a DECA Role-Play Works** — BENCHMARK LESSON (revised v2, full text in doc 10). The complete
  sequence: RECEIVE SCENARIO → IDENTIFY ROLES → IDENTIFY PROBLEM → READ PERFORMANCE INDICATORS →
  PREPARE → OPEN → RECOMMEND → JUSTIFY → IMPLEMENT → MEASURE → ANSWER QUESTIONS → CLOSE → RECEIVE
  SCORE SHEET, plus one full annotated weak/strong role-play.

### Module 1 — Reading the Situation
*Outcome: can decode a prompt and plan within the official prep window. Prerequisite: Module 0.
Applies to: role-play families. Classification: shared foundation.*

- **1. Understanding Your Role and the Judge's Role** — who you are, who the judge is playing, and why
  every sentence is addressed to that character. ⟨REVISED — B-6⟩ Adds the **judge-reality caveat**:
  judges are frequently volunteers, sometimes first-time, sometimes distracted. The
  "business executive" framing is a *model*, not a guarantee — build a response that survives a judge
  who is not tracking closely.
- **2. Reading and Decoding the Scenario** — the 5-extract method (my role / judge's role / problem /
  PIs / constraints). ⟨REVISED⟩ **Practice emphasises decoding under time pressure**, which is the
  reported bottleneck — not comprehension difficulty. Guided decode → timed decode → redo.
- **3. Understanding Performance Indicators** — ⟨D6⟩ PIs are **behaviors to demonstrate, never to
  recite**; judge instructions score only the listed PIs, and **PIs are not announced in advance.**
  Teaches the PI method: PI → PLAIN MEANING → SCENARIO REQUIREMENT → IN-CHARACTER ACTION →
  JUDGE-FACING EXPLANATION → MEASUREMENT.
  ⟨B-2 — UNRESOLVED, taught as a judgment call⟩ **Whether to name a PI's title aloud or weave it into
  the conversation is genuinely contested.** Teach **both options with their tradeoffs**; never present
  one as the answer. Official rule is *demonstrate* and is **silent on style**; an official page
  describing DECA's own sample presentations notes there is not a single right or wrong way to present
  — general support for avoiding one rigid script, and **not** evidence that judges treat the two
  styles equally. **Advisor/judge validation required.**
  ⟨B-7 — THIN EVIDENCE, labeled⟩ **Behavior-type PIs** ("Demonstrate…") get their own short drill.
  Evidence base is 2 discussions, below saturation — labeled as thin.
- **4. Using Preparation Time** — the 10 sourced minutes for Individual Series. ⟨REVISED — B-1⟩ Adds a
  **minute-by-minute prep budget**, a **hard note-length constraint (cue words, roughly three words
  per PI — not sentences)**, and a **filled example prep page.** Note bloat, not comprehension, is the
  reported failure mode. ⟨D19⟩ Materials are event-provided and prep notes are usable in the role-play
  — per the current official rules.

### Module 2 — Performing the Meeting
*Outcome: can deliver a five-part recommendation in character. Prerequisite: Module 1.
Applies to: role-play families. Classification: shared foundation.*

- **5. Entering Character** — the switch from student to professional.
- **6. Opening the Meeting** — agenda-setting in two sentences.
- **7. Identifying the Business Problem** — surface complaint vs underlying business problem.
- **8. Building Specific Recommendations** — concrete actions with scope, owner, and timing.
- **9. Business Reasoning** — attaching the because: revenue, cost, retention, loyalty, brand, risk.
- **10. Implementation and Feasibility** — who does what by when with what resources.
- **11. Cost, Risk, and Tradeoffs** — naming what your plan costs and what could go wrong.
- **12. Measuring Success** — the one or two metrics the business would actually watch.
- **13. Using Your Prep Materials** ⟨RETITLED — D2⟩ — what you may bring, create, and refer to during
  the meeting. **Role-play families prohibit bringing visual aids**; only materials created during
  preparation are allowed. Visual aids proper belong to the prepared-event families and are out of
  scope here.

### Module 3 — Pressure and Close
*Outcome: can answer the judge's questions without fabricating and read their own score sheet.
Prerequisite: Module 2. Applies to: role-play families. Classification: shared foundation.*

- **14. Handling Judge Questions** ⟨REVISED — D4⟩ — **the official flow is an uninterrupted
  presentation, then questions — not interruption.** By family:
  - **Individual Series / Principles / PFL** — **scripted standard questions, identical for every
    competitor**, followed by clarifications.
  - **Team Decision Making** — conditional questioning plus clarifications.
  - **Professional Selling and Consulting (PSC)** — **PSC has no scripted standard questions; the
    judge may ask appropriate questions if time remains.** Do not imply that every PSC interaction
    includes questions, and do not infer a universal scoring consequence.

  Answer frame: direct answer → reason → tie back to the recommendation; never collapsing.
  ⟨NEW — B-9⟩ **Non-fabricating response template** for a question you cannot answer: give the direct
  honest answer, name the method you would use to find out, and offer the follow-up. The community
  norm of inventing an answer is actively bad practice and is displaced here.
- **15. Closing Persuasively** — summarizing recommendations + impact + confident ask.
- **16. Score-Sheet Literacy** ⟨NEW — B-5/D16⟩ — read your own evaluation form: what the categories
  are, what each rewards, and how practice feedback maps onto them. **Official rubric anchors:
  Solution (Unique/Accurate, Practical, Effective), the Career Competencies triad, and the
  Novice→Exemplary anchor language.** A ballot is not a rubric; the official form is.

## DECA Preparation Sheet (taught in L4, reused everywhere)

One consistent layout, drilled until automatic. **Cue words only — not sentences.**

```
TOP:    My role | Judge's role | Main problem (one sentence)
LEFT:   Performance Indicators (as actions to demonstrate) — ~3-word cues each
CENTER: Recommendation 1 → business reason → implementation → measurement
RIGHT:  Recommendation 2 → business reason → implementation → measurement
BOTTOM: Opening line | Likely judge questions (2–3) | Closing line
```

## Performance-Indicator rule (non-negotiable in content and feedback rubrics)

⟨D6⟩ Saying "My first performance indicator is customer relations" earns nothing **on its own**. Every
PI must be **demonstrated** through: a concrete decision → business reasoning → implementation →
feasibility → measurement. All DECA feedback rubrics encode this; the AI judge/coach never rewards
PI-naming as a substitute for demonstration.

⟨B-2⟩ This rule is about **demonstration versus recitation**. It is **not** a ruling on whether to
speak a PI's title aloud — that remains an open judgment call (Module 1, L3).

## The five-part recommendation scaffold — CompeteReady's, not DECA's

⟨D7 — CRITICAL LABELING HONESTY⟩ **PROBLEM → RECOMMENDATION → BUSINESS REASON → IMPLEMENTATION →
MEASUREMENT is CompeteReady's teaching scaffold. It is not official DECA terminology.** It is designed
to map onto the official evaluation-form categories:

| Our scaffold step | Official anchor it serves |
|---|---|
| Problem | Correctly identifies the situation the PIs address |
| Recommendation | **Solution — Unique/Accurate** |
| Business reason | **Solution — Effective**; Career Competencies (reasoning) |
| Implementation | **Solution — Practical** |
| Measurement | **Solution — Practical / Effective** (testability) |

⟨B-8 — CONSTRAINED⟩ **Implementation and measurement are concrete ways to make a recommendation more
practical, testable, and aligned with the official Solution criteria. They may strengthen a response.
This research did not establish that they reliably separate top scores, and no score improvement is
promised.** Advisor/judge validation required.

⟨B-3 — OPTIONAL GLOSSARY CONTEXT ONLY⟩ Some competitors use **D-E-C-A-style mnemonics** for handling a
performance indicator. **Expansions differ between communities and none is official.** This is
recorded as optional glossary context so learners recognise the term. **CompeteReady's five-part
scaffold remains separate and clearly labeled as ours; the community acronym is never assessed and
never appears in the core rubric.**

## Knowledge-to-Performance Bridge (business terms)

Every major business term eventually carries 7 fields, authored:

1. **Definition** (plain, current)
2. **Exam recognition** (how it's typically probed on the cluster exam)
3. **Scenario meaning** (what it looks like inside a role-play situation)
4. **What the character would DO** (the in-character decision)
5. **How the character EXPLAINS it to the judge** (one model sentence, natural speech — never the
   textbook definition recited)
6. **How it is implemented** (who/what/when)
7. **How success is measured** (the metrics)

Worked example (authoring standard):

> **TERM: Customer retention**
> Definition: keeping existing customers and encouraging repeat business.
> In-character use: "As the guest-experience manager, I would provide immediate service recovery and
> send a personalized follow-up offer. This addresses the current complaint while protecting the
> long-term relationship."
> Measurement: repeat booking rate, satisfaction scores, referral activity, offer redemption.

Drill type built on the bridge ("PI-to-action drill", doc 04): given a term or PI, the learner writes
the in-character sentence (fields 4–5) — not the definition.

## DECA Knowledge Mastery

⟨D8/D15⟩ Tagging (target state — additive metadata; **requires approval before any schema change**):
career cluster · instructional area · **performance indicator keyed by official code** · concept ·
difficulty · error category · seen/unseen status.

**PI-tracking caveats (official):** store **(code, cluster, list-year)** as the membership key; PI
lists are **re-baselined each July**; rewordings must be flagged rather than silently merged; exam
weighting is published at the **instructional-area level only** — never per PI.

⟨B-11⟩ Practice-exam pools exhaust and inflate scores. The study planner is **blueprint-driven**, and
(code, cluster, list-year) versioning is surfaced to the learner so a stale PI list is visible.

Improvement loop: MISSED QUESTION → IDENTIFY PI/CONCEPT → TRACK RECURRENCE → TARGETED MICRO-LESSON →
FOCUSED DRILL → UNSEEN TRANSFER QUESTION → SPACED RETEST.

Seen vs unseen accuracy are SEPARATE numbers. Mastery claims require unseen accuracy.

## Conference and level literacy

⟨D21/B-12⟩ **Dress and conference requirements are taught by level, never universally.** The official
blazer requirement is documented **at ICDC**, where it applies when appearing before judges and for
stage recognition; business attire applies for briefing and testing under the current Guide;
**chartered associations may enforce differently.** **There is no universal blazer rule**, and no
device deduction, cut line, normalization mechanic, advancement model, or entry limit is ever taught
as universal.

## DECA Quiz Bowl plan

**Corpus status: the Quiz Bowl document is NOT in this repository.** Nothing below is buildable until
the user delivers it. When delivered, every item passes the intake pipeline before any use:

EXTRACT → DEDUPLICATE → FACT-CHECK → UPDATE (current practice/taxonomy) → MAP to current DECA
instructional areas/PIs → REWRITE ORIGINALLY → EXPLAIN (why the answer is right, why distractors are
wrong) → CONNECT to role-play application (bridge fields 3–7).

Items failing fact-check or unmappable to current taxonomy are dropped, not patched. The document is
a concept corpus only — never treated as a current official exam bank, never assumed correct, never
copied.

⟨D20⟩ **Officially released sample exams (published per family with PI-coded answer keys) are a
legitimate official practice-item source**, distinct from the undelivered Quiz Bowl document.
**Licensed or member-gated DECA content is never scraped** (doc 00 §7).

Four future modes (build order = listed order):

1. **Learn** — question + answer + full explanation; wrong options explained.
2. **Recall** — same concepts, no options (free recall, testing effect).
3. **Buzz-In** — prompt revealed progressively. Timed, game-feel, still real mastery data.
4. **Apply** — the bridge closed: the learner writes the in-character sentence; feedback via the
   authored bridge rubric.
