# 01 — Debate Course Map (complete)

Status: **DRAFT V2** — revised per the approved Final Research Synthesis + Correction Addendum
(dispositions C1–C22, A1–A16, V-1–V-11). 7 modules + Practice Lab.
The approved Claim/Warrant/Impact lesson and its mastery wiring are **PRESERVED** — it remains
Lesson 5 with additive changes only (see Module 2).

**Source-tier legend** — every lesson carries one of these tags in the next revision layer:
**OFFICIAL** (directly required by a current official source) · **STABLE-TEACHING** (durable
instructional material, never a rules source) · **TIER-2** (established instructional practice or
learning science; CompeteReady's design). Where a term differs across formats, the format is named
inline.

**Format scope.** This course is grounded in **Public Forum**, which is NSDA-governed and sourced.
**Parliamentary debate is NOT NSDA-governed and has no official source in the reviewed corpus.** It
appears only as an explicitly flagged placeholder in Lesson 1; **no parliamentary structure, timing,
or rule may be taught until a dedicated sourcing pass (NPDA/state leagues) is completed.**

Format per lesson: **Title — objective; worked-example core; practice type.** Every lesson follows the
13-step permanent sequence (doc 00 §1 / spec §3): explain → show process → expert model → weak version →
compare → annotate → guided practice → learner production → feedback on their words → immediate redo →
transfer mini-scenario → spaced review with a different example → one next lesson.

Progression across the course: **Learn → Practice → Apply → Compete.**

## Module 1 — Understanding the Event

*Outcome: can name every speech, flow a short speech, and walk into a first tournament without
procedural surprise. Prerequisite: none. Classification: shared foundation + competition orientation.*

- **0. How a Debate Round Works** — the full round from resolution to RFD, using the SOURCED PF
  structure from the registry (4-min constructives, 3-min crossfires, 4-min rebuttals, 3-min summaries,
  grand crossfire, 2-min final focus, 3-min prep). ⟨REVISED⟩ Adds the **coin toss** (winner picks side
  OR speaking order). ⟨REVISED⟩ The final-focus new-argument expectation is taught as a **dated ballot
  instruction observed on official ballots — not as a universal current rule.** Worked example: a
  compressed annotated round on one motion. Practice: order-the-round + "what does this speech have to
  do?" identify set.
- **1. Understanding Debate Formats** — what this course trains and what it does not. ⟨REVISED⟩ States
  honestly that **parliamentary is not NSDA-governed and its specification requires separate
  sourcing**; other NSDA formats exist and are out of scope. Practice: given a described situation,
  name the format and what changes.
- **2. Flowing Primer** ⟨NEW — A1⟩ — the minimum viable flow before any lesson that depends on it: a
  two-column sheet, one column per speech, responses written beside the argument they answer, gaps as
  drops. Deliberately small; the deep lesson is Module 5. Practice: flow a 60-second authored speech,
  compare against a model flow.
- **3. Your First Tournament** ⟨NEW — A6⟩ — the procedural day: registration, pairings, rooms, what to
  bring, what happens between rounds, what a ballot is and when you see it. Labeled **TIER-2 —
  CompeteReady orientation, outside the official corpus.** Practice: a first-day checklist the learner
  fills for their own tournament.
- **4. What Judges Are Deciding** — the judge votes on argument comparison, not vibes. ⟨REVISED⟩
  Grounded in current Paradigm Standards (decisions on personal beliefs overturned; reasoned
  decision), ballot RFD prompts, and citizen-judge framing. ⟨REVISED⟩ **Speaker points are taught as
  the official ballots' 20–30 descriptor scale, with sub-20 reserved as an unethical-conduct flag —
  the earlier "0–30" phrasing was incorrect and is withdrawn.** Worked example: one clash, two judge
  write-ups. Practice: read a mini-round and predict the RFD focus.
- **5. Advocacy Is a Role, Not a Personal Confession** — the advocate role; commitment without
  personal endorsement; why judges score advocacy quality, not sincerity.
- **6. Defending a Side You Do Not Personally Support** — the core ethic:
  SEPARATE BELIEF FROM ROLE → UNDERSTAND THE POSITION → STEELMAN IT → SUPPORT IT ACCURATELY →
  ANTICIPATE OBJECTIONS → ADVOCATE WITH COMMITMENT. Explicit never-list: no lying, no invented
  evidence, no pretending private belief, no misrepresenting the opponent.
- **7. Watch and Judge a Full Round** ⟨NEW — C11⟩ — module capstone **activity**: watch a complete
  round, flow it, and write a ballot/RFD, then compare against a model RFD. ⟨CONSTRAINT⟩ Implemented
  with **CompeteReady-authored material or a lawful link to an official round** — never an embedded
  or re-hosted third-party asset, and never dependent on third-party caption availability (doc 00 §2).

## Module 2 — Argument Construction

*Outcome: can build a contention whose warrant is a mechanism, and can source and cite evidence to the
official standard. Prerequisite: Module 1. Classification: shared foundation.*

- **8. Claim, Warrant, and Impact** — the EXISTING approved CWI lesson, **preserved**; mastery wiring
  untouched (`debate-claim-building`). ⟨REVISED — attribution⟩ **Cited specifically to the Debate
  Training Guide.** ⟨REVISED — honesty⟩ The reviewed official corpus **also contains compatible
  four-part Claim/Data/Warrant/Impact presentations**; the lesson must **not imply NSDA publishes only
  one formulation.** ⟨NEW — A7⟩ Adds the two-mental-models warning (reasoning-that-proves-the-claim vs
  bridge-from-evidence-to-claim) and the "your sentences are all claims" diagnostic. ⟨NEW — V-1⟩ Adds
  a **second weak-example type: a citation-heavy argument with an under-explained speaker-articulated
  mechanism** — heavily sourced, fluent, and still missing the reasoning. **This example must never be
  described as automatically warrantless**; captions cannot establish whether a cited source carried
  the warrant.
- **9. Building a Causal Chain** — claim → mechanism → outcome links; each arrow must be defended.
- **10. Finding Missing Logical Links** — spotting the unstated premise.
- **11. Researching and Finding Evidence** ⟨NEW — C10/A8⟩ — **search craft first**: where evidence
  lives, how to find it, how to judge a source before cutting it. Anecdotally the real bottleneck is
  *finding*, not cutting.
- **12. Evidence Quality and Source Credibility** ⟨SPLIT — C9/A16, part 1⟩ — recency, expertise, bias,
  methodology vs anecdote; explaining evidence beats reading it. Weak/strong: same card deployed lazily
  vs explained. **TIER-2 heuristics.**
- **13. Evidence Rules and Citation Standards** ⟨SPLIT — C9/A16, part 2 — NEW⟩ — the **OFFICIAL** layer:
  oral citation (author + year), written citation elements, the paraphrase standard, and evidence
  challenge/penalty awareness. ⟨A16⟩ **Rule and norm are taught as separate layers** so learners never
  mistake a circuit norm for a rule.
- **14. Correlation Versus Causation** — the three alternate explanations.
- **15. Argument Mapping** — draw the claim/support/objection structure; find the load-bearing node.
- **16. Building a Complete Contention** — assemble the module into one full contention. ⟨REVISED —
  C14⟩ Adds official case vocabulary: framework, taglines, 2–3 contentions, signposting. ⟨REVISED —
  V-7⟩ **Each contention's causal path must connect clearly to an impact; several supporting reasons
  may legitimately converge on one shared impact.** Module capstone: guided build → judge questions →
  redo → unseen-motion transfer.

Learner must reliably distinguish claim / evidence / warrant / mechanism / impact. Warrants are taught
as actual logical connections — "because"-content that makes the claim follow — never as "a longer
explanation."

## Module 3 — Persuasion

*Outcome: can diagnose a judge type and re-render the same argument for it. Prerequisite: Module 2.
Classification: shared foundation; **TIER-2 grounding** except where noted.*

- **17. Ethos, Pathos, and Logos** — BENCHMARK LESSON, fully authored in doc 09. **TIER-2.**
- **18. How People Are Actually Persuaded** — prior beliefs, trust, coherence, vividness.
- **19. Framing** — the same policy as safety-story vs freedom-story; choosing the comparison class.
- **20. Stories, Examples, and Analogies** — concrete instance + representative data as a pair.
- **21. Conceding to Build Credibility** ⟨RETITLED — C20⟩ — the persuasive use of concession: conceding
  the trivial to buy credibility for the decisive.
- **22. Judge Adaptation: Diagnosis** ⟨SPLIT — A4, part 1 — NEW⟩ — read the judge: paradigm, ballot
  language, experience signals, what this judge will actually reward. **OFFICIAL grounding** (Paradigm
  Standards, citizen-judge framing). ⟨CONSTRAINT⟩ The split rests on official and anecdotal evidence;
  **video observation did not support it and is not cited here.**
- **23. Judge Adaptation: Execution** ⟨SPLIT — A4, part 2⟩ — rewrite one argument three times for a
  community judge, an evidence-focused judge, and a skeptical judge. This is the audience-adaptation
  drill.
- **24. Ethical Persuasion Versus Manipulation** — framing honest tradeoffs vs hiding them.

## Module 4 — Reasoning Failures

*Outcome: can find and repair a broken inference in their own writing. Prerequisite: Module 2.
Classification: shared foundation; **TIER-2**; **validation-gated (coach).***

⟨COMPRESSED — A11⟩ The 10-lesson fallacy sequence is consolidated. Evidence for compression is a
**recurring absence of learner demand** — a weak evidence type — so the compression is **provisional
pending coach validation**, and the repair capstone is retained regardless. Official pedagogy reaches
the same goal through warrant analysis.

Method for EVERY lesson here (never reward mere naming):
IDENTIFY THE CLAIM → FIND THE UNSUPPORTED LINK → EXPLAIN WHY THE CONCLUSION DOES NOT FOLLOW →
RESPOND DIRECTLY → REPAIR THE SAME ERROR IN YOUR OWN ARGUMENT.

- **25. What a Logical Fallacy Actually Is** — a broken inference, not a magic word; why shouting
  "strawman!" scores nothing; fallacy vs merely weak argument.
- **26. Unsupported and Overreaching Inferences** — consolidated: hasty generalization, slippery slope,
  false dilemma.
- **27. Misrepresentation and Misdirection** — consolidated: straw man, ad hominem, cherry-picking.
- **28. Authority and Circularity** — consolidated: appeal to authority, circular reasoning.
- **29. Fixing Fallacies in Your Own Case** ⟨CAPSTONE — RETAINED⟩ — the learner's Module-2 contention is
  audited for their own fallacies; classify, repair, redo.

## Module 5 — Clash and Round Strategy

*Outcome: can flow a full round, choose responses, weigh, and collapse to a ballot story.
Prerequisite: Modules 1–2; **the Module-1 flowing primer is required**. Classification: shared
foundation.*

- **30. Flowing (deep)** ⟨REVISED — C15⟩ — preflow, column-per-speech, responses beside the argument,
  drops as gaps, tagline vs evidence, two-color method. ⟨NEW — V-8, optional⟩ **Author-name indexing is
  offered as one optional signposting technique**, useful when an evidence dispute centres on a
  specific source — **argument labels and response substance matter more than recalling author names.**
  Practice: flow a short authored speech; compare to a model flow.
- **31. Four-Step Refutation** — THEY SAY → BUT → BECAUSE → THEREFORE.
- **32. Choosing the Best Response** ⟨REVISED — C12⟩ — official typology folded in: offense vs defense,
  indict vs turn (link turns, impact turns, the double-turn warning), warrant-level vs impact-level.
  Advanced frame: IDENTIFY → ANSWER → EXPLAIN → COMPARE.
- **33. Rebuilding After an Attack (Frontlining)** — answer the attack, then re-extend with its impact.
- **34. Blocks and Frontline Preparation** ⟨NEW — A9⟩ — converting live composition into preparation:
  building reusable answer blocks and frontlines as an explicit **output** of study, not an
  improvisation skill. ⟨CONSTRAINT⟩ Whether competitors pre-script is **not observable**; this is
  taught as a preparation method, not a claim about what others do.
- **35. Cross-Examination and Crossfire** ⟨REVISED — C13/A2⟩ — official mechanics: **crossfire is
  question-and-answer, not an independently voted speech**; the first-speaking team asks first; grand
  crossfire is seated; ask questions with purpose. ⟨A2⟩ Corrects the "win crossfire" mental model and
  teaches **cashing your concessions in the next speech.** ⟨V-5 — optional, MEDIUM⟩ Explicitly naming
  where a concession came from is offered as **one drillable option**; its usefulness is **not
  established by observation and requires coach/judge validation** before being presented as preferred
  practice. Worked example: an annotated crossfire.
- **36. Collapsing to Your Winning Path** ⟨RETITLED — C20⟩ — the strategic use of concession: kicking
  arguments, choosing the winning path.
- **37. Weighing** ⟨REVISED — A12/V-3/V-4⟩ — **teach WHEN weighing matters before the mechanism zoo.**
  Magnitude, probability, timeframe and reversibility are taught as **analytic categories, not required
  speech vocabulary** — competent rounds are observed to weigh without ever saying those words.
  ⟨V-4⟩ The **"even if" construction is taught as one useful comparative move** (granting the
  opponent's point, then comparing) — **not as a universal speaking norm.** Weak: two unweighed impacts
  pass in the night. Strong: an explicit comparison.
- **38. Crystallization and Voting Issues** ⟨REVISED — C17/A3/V-6⟩ — **collapse to the minimum number of
  decisive issues needed for a coherent ballot story — which may be one issue or several, depending on
  the round.** Adds the official summary-frontlines vs rebuttal-attacks role division, and the
  ballot-statement close as one observed useful structure. ⟨CONSTRAINT⟩ Any final-focus new-argument
  guidance is stated as a **dated ballot instruction, not a universal current rule.**
- **39. Writing a Reason for Decision** — the learner writes an RFD for an authored round; compared
  against a model RFD.

## Module 7 — Performance

*Outcome: can deliver with control and diagnose their own delivery. Prerequisite: Module 6.
Classification: advanced.*

- **48. Delivery Diagnosis** ⟨NEW — A10⟩ — before any delivery drill, name the actual problem: rate,
  enunciation, cadence, or **writing too much to say naturally**. "Speak slower" is the most common and
  least actionable ballot note; this lesson makes it actionable. ⟨CONSTRAINT⟩ **No numeric
  words-per-minute target is published anywhere.** Caption-derived rate proxies are not measurements,
  are not stable across segment-boundary choices, and **must never be surfaced to learners.** Any rate
  target requires coach/judge validation.
- **49. Debate as Performance** — same speech text, weak vs strong delivery, so delivery is the only
  variable.
- **50. Vocal Commitment** · **51. Pace, Pauses, and Emphasis** · **52. Eye Contact and Body Language** ·
  **53. Sounding Confident Without Exaggerating** · **54. Speaking Naturally Instead of Reading** ·
  **55. Recovering During a Speech.**
- **56. Video Self-Review** — staged protocol: audio only → muted video → full recording → immediate
  corrected re-recording. One focus per pass, notes tied to timestamps. **Learner-generated recording
  carries the privacy gates in doc 00 §8; no identifiable upload is ever required.**
- **57. Guided Full Round** — full round with Side Coach available, judge feedback mapped to lesson
  numbers.
- **58. Independent Full Round** — full round, no assistance, real ballot; weaknesses route into the
  recommendation engine (doc 04). **Compete stage.** Module and course capstone.
